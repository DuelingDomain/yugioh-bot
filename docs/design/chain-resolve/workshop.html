Chain resolve panel: say what is resolving, and what happened

Phase 1 design. Every frame uses the app's duel tokens, the three duel fonts and real card text from cards.cdb. The board is a stand-in drawn at the real proportions. Gold = activating, violet = resolving, red = negated; a resolved link goes quiet grey.

1. 1v1, wide room (1920): a real gutter, the full panel gutter 320

A three-link chain, mid-resolution. The opponent's Trap Hole (CL1) answers your Normal Summon of Gaia. You answer with Seven Tools of the Bandit (CL2), the opponent answers with Solemn Judgment (CL3). CL3 has resolved and negated CL2. CL1 is resolving now: card art, name, owner, what it does, what it targets, and the result as soon as it is known. The stack below keeps each link's state with a one-line result, in resolution order (CL3 first).

2. 1v1 at 1440 x 900 measured gutter 164

I measured the real 1v1 board in the FX lab at a room-sized stage: 164 px at 1440, 142 px at 1280, 320 px at 1920. Today the stack is already numbered chips below 170 px, so at 1440 the owner sees no panel at all. The new narrow form fits 156 px: the same content, art on top. Under about 150 px (1280 and below) it becomes the strip (section 5).

3. A single effect always answerable: "what just happened?"

A lone Spell, Trap or ignition effect is a chain of one in the engine (chain_size 1), so it already flows through the same events. The panel says Effect, not "Chain Link 1 of 1" (the header and the stack disappear), shows the specific effect string when the card has one (Raigeki does), and then holds the result for a short recap after chain-end. The hold is new (about 3.2 s at 1x, scaled by the speed slider, 0.8 s with reduced motion) and never delays play.

4. The hero states, and what a prompt does to the panel

The hero changes only its tone and its last line. The hero link also stays in the stack (number, name, state only) so the whole chain is always visible. Left to right: a link just activated (gold, shows what it will do), resolving (violet), negated (red, name struck through, says by what), resolved with a result (quiet), and folded: when a prompt surface would meet the column, the panel does not vanish into chips, it drops to the strip first.

5. Phone, 390 wide strip + tap to expand

The strip is two lines in the slot the numbered chips use today: top right of the board, beside the opponent's LP plate. It dodges LP plates and prompt panels with the same placement code. It is the only tappable thing in the layer: a real button, 44 px tall with a 48 px hit area, outside the aria-hidden part of the tree. Tap to open the same panel as a sheet pinned under the strip; tap the scrim, the sheet or the strip again to close. The sheet never reaches below an open prompt.

6. Tables and Rooftop (2v2) same strip, wider, priority chips stay under it

On a 3-way, 4-way or Tag board the chips today are a wide pill at the top left with "You, Rook, Juniper, Mirelle" priority chips. The strip takes that corner and the priority chips keep their row under it. Names read by player name, never "Opponent". The prompt's own chain list (PromptCenter) is left alone; the two never duplicate: the strip says what is resolving, the prompt lists what you can respond with.
