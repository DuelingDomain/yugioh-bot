Feedback board: three approaches

Three simpler ways to build the feedback section. All three share one model: an idea is open for votes, then in progress, then done, and the people who backed it hear about it once. The differences are in how the board looks and feels. Each approach shows five key screens, how it covers each requirement, and what it would cost to build. The comparison and the open questions come last.

What you asked for

A feedback section inside the web app, run from the existing owner admin console. The alpha is small and invitation-only (41 members), so every approach favours one-tap actions and a short owner queue. The requirement codes below are reused in every approach and in the comparison.

The shared model. A new idea waits for your approval, visible only to its author. Approved ideas are open for votes. You tap Start and it shows In progress. You tap Done and everyone who voted it up gets one notice. There are no steps, dates or update posts to keep current.

Approach A

The Ladder

A feedback board is a leaderboard for ideas: the most backed climb, and every player already knows how to read one.

Ideas stand in one ranked list, the same shape as the duel leaderboard. Vote up or down and the row slides to its new place. The two ideas being built sit in a short band above the ladder, and Done is a roll of honour on its own tab.

The model

  1. Waiting

    A player posts it. Only the author and the owner see it, as an unranked row.

  2. Open

    On the ladder with a rank. Anyone who plays votes up or down.

  3. In progress

    Pinned above the ladder with one label and nothing else.

  4. Done

    On the Done tab. Everyone who voted up, and the author, gets one notice.

  • The owner's whole job is three taps: Approve or Decline, Start, Done.
  • Team posts skip Waiting. They go straight to Open with a gold Team label.
  • Declined ideas leave the board. The author sees a short reason, such as "Already on the board".
  • Rank is up votes minus down votes.Ties go to the idea with more up votes. Both counts show on every row.
  • Voting moves the row.Tap and the idea slides to its new place, with the ranks it passed shifting by one.
  • Tap an area tag to see one area.The six rank ideas appear together under Ranked. Their numbers stay the same.

Trade-offs

Strengths

  • Players already read a leaderboard, so rank and votes need no help text.
  • A vote has a visible payoff: the idea climbs, and the prompt says how far.
  • The owner keeps nothing up to date. Three taps move an idea along.

Risks

  • Early votes decide the top, and a new idea starts at the bottom.
  • One long list gets hard to scan as the board grows past a few dozen ideas.
  • Net score hides a split idea: Quick reactions (6 up, 5 down) sits at the bottom at 1.

Build size

S

One page with a sort, one admin view with three lists, a notice and a prompt slot on the result screens. No steps, dates or update posts to store.

Assumes and asks

What it assumes

  • The app can show an in-app notice, such as under the bell.
  • A vote can be changed or taken back at any time.
  • Rank is worked out when the page loads, so nothing needs updating by hand.
  • Area tags are a fixed list of eight.

Questions it raises

  • Should players keep voting on an idea once it is In progress?
  • Should a player need to finish one activity before voting?
  • Should Done ideas stay visible on the ladder for a day?
  • Is net votes the right rank, or only up votes?

Approach B

One pick at a time

A feedback board is a draft pack: one idea in front of you, one vote, then the next.

The board opens on a single idea card. Vote up or down and the next card slides in, the way a draft pick does. See all ideas shows the same ideas as a list. Two slim shelves under the card hold In progress and Done, and that is all the progress there is.

The model

  1. Player postsWaitingOnly the author and the owner see it. Team ideas skip this.
  2. Owner taps ApproveOpenOn the board. Players vote up or down, one card at a time or in a list.
  3. Owner taps StartIn progressOne chip on a shelf. No steps, dates or percentages.
  4. Owner taps DoneDoneEveryone who voted up and the author get one notice. The idea moves to the Done shelf.

Declined ideas leave the board. The author sees a short reason, picked from a preset or typed as one line.

  • The deck holds the ideas you have not voted on. A vote slides the next card in; Skip tucks a card to the back.
  • See all ideas lists everything, your votes marked. A vote in either view counts once.
  • Votes toggle: tap the same arrow again to take it back.
  • The owner moves an idea with three taps in the console. Done can carry a one-line note and never needs one.

Trade-offs

Strengths

  • A first-time player needs no help: one card, two buttons.
  • Every idea gets in front of every player, so quiet ideas collect votes too.
  • The prompt after a duel is the same card as the board, so the board fills as people play.

Risks

  • The deck hides the size of the board until someone taps All ideas.
  • Card order decides who gets votes first; a bad order starves the later cards.
  • Fast taps make drive-by down votes easy, with no reason attached.

Build size

S

One votes table, one state field, three console actions and one notice. The deck and the list read the same rows, so the card is a view, not a feature.

Assumes and asks

What it assumes

  • The board stays small enough (tens of ideas) for a deck to feel complete.
  • Players are fine voting on ideas they have not asked for.
  • In progress ideas show their counts but stop taking votes.

Questions it raises

  • What order should the deck use: random per player, fewest votes first, or Team ideas first?
  • Should the deck bring a skipped card back later in the same visit?
  • Should players see down-vote counts, or only up-vote counts?

Approach C

The Field

A feedback board is a play mat. Read it left to right: ideas, what we are building, what is done.

One mat with three zones. Ideas are cards on the left. The few cards being built sit face up in the middle. Finished work stacks into a pile on the right. When the owner taps Start or Done, a card slides one zone along, so the whole state of the board shows in one look.

The model

Waiting

You postOnly you and the owner see it. Team posts skip this.

Open

Owner taps ApproveAnyone who plays can vote up or down.

In progress

Owner taps StartOne label. Nothing to keep updating.

Done

Owner taps DoneEveryone who voted up gets one notice.

The mat: Ideas, In progress, Done

  1. A post waits for the owner. Only its author and the owner can see a Waiting card. A declined idea leaves, and the author reads a short reason.
  2. Ideas is the open zone. Every card shows a title, one line, an area, a Team label if it has one, and votes. Vote up or down, and tap again to take it back.
  3. Start moves a card to the middle. The only thing it says is In progress. There are no steps, dates or posts to maintain.
  4. Done moves it to the pile. Voters who went up, and the author, get one notice: “Done: title. You backed this.” The owner can add a one-line note.

Trade-offs

Strengths

  • The whole board is one look: three zones, each with its count, and the middle zone is never empty of meaning.
  • Each owner action is one visible card move, so the model teaches itself.
  • Nothing to maintain. The owner never writes an update.

Risks

  • The mat needs width. On a phone it becomes three tabs and loses the one-look.
  • The Ideas zone scrolls once it holds more than about ten cards.
  • Wide cards show fewer ideas at once than a plain list does.

Build size

S

One page, one card component, one vote control, one form and three admin lists. Notices reuse the existing in-app notice. No schedules, steps or update posts.

Assumes and asks

What it assumes

  • The app sidebar can sit collapsed to icons, as it does today, so the mat gets room.
  • An in-app notice already exists to carry the Done message.
  • The area list is fixed and short.

Questions it raises

  • Should voting stay open on In progress cards?
  • Do the shipped lines from before the board belong in the Done pile?
  • Is sort by votes enough, or should players switch to newest first?
  • Does the author count as a backer when the Done notice goes out?

My read

A is the easiest to read and the most familiar: players already know leaderboards, and it holds up as ideas pile up. Its weak spot is that new ideas start at the bottom. B fixes exactly that. My recommendation: A's ladder as the board, with B's one-card pick as the after-game prompt and as a "Vote on new ideas" button at the top of the ladder. C is the prettiest on a wide screen but loses its point on phones. Question 1 below is where we decide.

Open questions, in the order we'll go through them

The first question picks the direction. The rest apply whichever approach wins, and each one changes what gets built.

  1. Which direction?

    Pick one approach, or a blend that borrows one idea from another.

    A · The LadderB · One pick at a timeC · The FieldA blend
  2. What is the board called?

    A and C name it "Next Turn", from round one. B calls it "Ideas", which needs no explaining.

    Next TurnIdeasSomething else
  3. Who counts as Team?

    Team posts skip approval and carry a Team label. Is that only you, or named helpers too?

    Only the ownerOwner plus named helpers
  4. Who may vote?

    The mocks let anyone vote once they have finished one duel, draft or tournament match, so votes come from people who play.

    After one finished activityAnyone signed in
  5. Show both vote counts, or one score?

    Showing "14 up · 1 down" is more honest about split ideas. One net score is simpler to scan.

    Both countsOne net score
  6. How often should the after-game ask appear?

    Asking after every game keeps the board full but can wear thin in a small alpha.

    After every gameAt most once a dayOnly when there's a new idea
  7. The rank system: what's real?

    The board shows all six ranked parts as open ideas. Is that right, or are some already decided?

    All six openSome go straight to In progressDrop some parts
  8. Author name by default?

    The mocks show the author's handle on their idea, with a switch to post without a name.

    Name on by defaultName off by default
  9. Starting state

    Is this right today? Card details on phones and Theme drafts In progress; Readable text and Format names open for votes; Daily puzzle left off the board.

    CorrectChange some
