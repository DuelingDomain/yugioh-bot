Tribute Summon, on the board

Press Tribute Summon and the game goes straight to your field. The monsters you can tribute already glow. Click one or two, and the summon happens. The small centred bar becomes a compact dock in the corner, and the tributes burn into light that pours into the new monster.

This page is the proposal for review. Nothing is built yet. Tokens are copied from the app (the Solid Vision duel shell: room.module.css, prompt-center.module.css, field.module.css). Card art loads from images.ygoprodeck.com, so you need to be online.

Recommendation, in five lines

  • Placement: bottom-right of the board, beside the hand. If the hand is too wide, it steps up to the row above the hand, right-aligned. Never bottom-left.
  • Auto-summon: yes. The moment the pick is complete and nothing more can be added, the answer goes out. The Summon button only appears when the pick is ambiguous (a monster that counts as 2, or more tributes than needed are allowed).
  • Cancel: always on screen while the engine allows it. Esc and right-click do the same. Click a picked monster to undo it.
  • Animation: lift, burn into 8 slices, an orb per tribute flies into the target zone, a short flash, then the existing heavy summon. 0.9 s before the summon starts, about the same as today's two Graveyard flights.
  • Opponent: sees the full animation (it comes from public events), never the dock or the glows.

What happens, step by step

  1. Open the card menu on a monster in your hand, choose Tribute Summon.
  2. The engine asks for tributes. No panel opens. Every monster that may be tributed lights up with the usual purple glow and tag. The dock appears in the corner: Tribute 1 monster · 0/1.
  3. Click a glowing monster. It lifts and turns gold (picked). The count moves.
  4. The pick is complete: the answer is sent at once. The dock goes away, the picked monsters stay gold until the tribute animation takes over (about 100 to 200 ms).
  5. Then the tribute animation, then the summon plays, then the usual zone prompt if the monster needs a zone.

Mistake? Click a picked monster to unpick it, press Esc, right-click, or press Cancel. All four go back to the action menu.

Level 5 or 6 monster: one tribute, 0/1

Three monsters glow, the fourth does not. The dim one cannot be tributed right now, so it has no glow and no tag. The dock is bottom-right, next to the hand and clear of the Extra and Deck piles. One click and the summon goes.

Level 7 or more: two tributes, 1/2

First pick is gold with a check. The counter reads 1/2 and the hint says what is missing. A second click meets the requirement with two cards, which is the maximum, so it sends at once. No Confirm.

A monster that counts as 2 Tributes

The ×2 tag shows before you click. Picking that monster alone meets 2/2, but you may still add another tribute, so the pick is ambiguous. The dock gains a gold Summon button. Press it, or press Enter.

2The dock, every state

Same family as the select bar: the navy surface, gold hairline and top bar, thumbnail of the monster being summoned, the chip that turns gold when the pick is complete. It is 250 px wide at 1440 px and scales with the board unit. The title says what to do, the chip says how far, the hint says what is missing.

Decision table: when does it send by itself?

need is the engine's minimum (a contribution value, not a card count). cap is the most cards you may pick. T is the contribution of the cards picked so far, n their count.

SituationExampleResult
T < needLevel 7, one plain monster pickedKeep picking. Chip shows T/need. No Summon button.

The answer is the same list of picked option ids as today ({ selected: [...] }), so the server needs no change. It already checks that the contribution reaches the minimum and that the count is at most the maximum.

3Where the dock sits

Real screenshots of the three shells, with the dock laid over them at real size. Blue dashed boxes are things the dock keeps clear of. The dock is part of the prompt layer, which already fills the board in every shell, so the same component serves all three. Phase 2 measures the hand and the plates and picks the first slot that is free.

1v1 room

Free slot beside the hand. The LP plate fills the bottom-left and the Deck and Graveyard piles stand above this slot, not in it. The chain list from the last release runs down the left gutter, so left is busy. The phase bar is below the board and does not collide.

3-way and 4-way table

Same slot, right of your hand. It sits under your LP plate and clear of the hand. On the 4-way table the layout is the same on your side.

2v2 Rooftop

Corners are taken here: your team LP plate is bottom-left and your partner's hand is bottom-right. The dock takes the fallback slot, centred on the row above your hand. That row is the Spell and Trap row, which a Tribute pick never uses.

Slot order (measured, first free wins)

  • 1. Right of the local hand, bottom of the board.
  • 2. Row above the hand, right-aligned (hand has 6+ cards, or a narrow board).
  • 3. Row above the hand, centred (Rooftop).
  • 4. Phone: full width on the row above the hand.

Each slot must not overlap a monster zone, an LP plate, another hand, or the chain list.

Why not bottom-left

  • The LP plate, the turn chip and the Extra pile live there.
  • The chain list is a left-gutter list; on narrow boards its chips wrap into the same corner.
  • Rooftop puts the team LP plate there.
  • On the right, the only neighbour is your Deck pile, which no Tribute pick needs.

AlternativeBottom-left dock, for comparison

Left works in the 1v1 room only if the LP plate stays on top of the chip row. It lands right on the plate and on the chain callout area. I do not recommend it.

How it is built (phase 2)

  • The moves right before a Tribute summon event of the same seat are the tributes (the history list already tags them this way). The planner marks them, so they skip the plain Graveyard flight.
  • Same overlay pattern as the destroy shards: ghost slices of the card at its zone, driven by the shared clock, so pace and speed settings apply.
  • DOM version only. The 3D layer is left alone, so there is no WebGL dependency for this.
  • Orbs and slices end on their own and never take pointer events.

What the opponent sees

The same animation, from public events. The opponent sees your tributes lift, burn and flow into the summon. Spectators and teammates see it too.

Before the answer

  • No glow, no tag, no dock on their screen. The picking prompt belongs to the seat that must answer.
  • Their status line stays as it is now: "Waiting for Alex."
  • They never learn which monsters were candidates, only the ones you sent.
  • If you cancel, nothing is sent. The opponent sees no change.

After the answer

  • Tributes run the animation above, then the summon plays.
  • The log line stays "Alex Tributed Dark Magician" as it is today.

Reduced motion

Nothing slides, nothing flies, no particles. Each tribute cross-fades through an amber wash in place (320 ms), the target zone shows a steady glow, the Graveyard count changes, and the summon runs its own reduced path (fade and glow). Same order, about 660 ms in total.

5Phone layout (390 px wide)

The dock is full width, on the row above your hand, with two stacked rows: the text, then big buttons (40 px high). The chip moves up beside the title and the hint line is dropped. Tap targets on the field are the zones themselves; the glow and tag make them easy to find. Touch has no right-click, so Cancel stays on screen.

6Other decisions

Double-tribute monsters

  • A gold ×2 pill on the card, shown with the glow, before the click.
  • The counter counts value, not cards: picking it alone moves 0/2 to 2/2. The ×2 pill on the card is the explanation, so the dock needs no hint.
  • The Summon button appears if a third card could still be added (see the decision table).

"Can't be tributed"

  • Not an option from the engine, so no glow, no tag.
  • Your own non-candidates dim to 50% during the pick, so the glowing ones stand out. The opponent's monsters stay as they are.
  • Click on one: a short red hint in the dock, "Can't be Tributed". The engine does not say why, so the text does not guess.

Keyboard and other shells

  • Number keys 1 to 9 pick the option in that position, as today. Enter presses Summon when it shows. Esc cancels.
  • The old modal path for tributes is removed. A tribute whose candidates are not all on the board (rare) keeps the existing card strip as its fallback. Other prompts are untouched.
  • Table and Rooftop: the same dock component, placed by the measured slots above. The 3D camera is not touched.

Open questions for you

  • If the number of candidates exactly equals the number required, should they be picked for you and the dock show only Summon? (Faster, but takes a choice away that is not a choice.) I left it off.
  • Button label: "Summon" (what happens) or "Confirm" (matches other bars)? I used "Summon".
